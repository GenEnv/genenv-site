Decoding Urban Form

Making zoning visible, testable, and open to more people.

Decoding Urban Form investigates how agentic AI can translate complex zoning regulations into accessible spatial knowledge. The platform reads bylaws, retrieves source clauses, structures conditional rules, and turns site-specific requirements into 3D massing proposals. Rather than relying on fixed parametric typologies, modeling agents work directly with parcel geometry and use deterministic measurements, multi-view rendering, and independent reviewer agents to inspect compliance and revise their designs.

Test bed — Vancouver

Vancouver serves as the project’s primary test bed, where complex regulations and discretionary planning processes make it difficult to understand how written rules translate into urban form. The research aims to improve regulatory literacy by helping designers, planners, developers, and community members explore development possibilities while preserving citations, uncertainty, and a traceable record of how each proposal was generated and reviewed.

Status & support

This work-in-progress project is currently supported by the Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council (SSHRC), with its initial development supported by the UBC Hampton Grant and UBC APSC PAEI Funding. The current stage focuses on developing and testing a conversational planning platform that connects regulatory interpretation, site information, and three-dimensional visualization.
