Computational Lexicalization

Turning design knowledge into callable language.

Computational Lexicalization proposes a way to make design knowledge legible to people and executable by machines. The research responds to a persistent gap in computational design: designers work through intention, judgment, and iterative exploration, while parametric tools require explicit instructions and formal logic.

Framework

The framework reorganizes design knowledge as a structured language composed of reusable lexicons and compositional grammars. Language models interpret intent and coordinate actions, while computational tools retain precision, editability, and traceable design logic. Rather than pursuing unrestricted machine autonomy, the research argues for bounded agency: designers define the available vocabulary, permissions, and rules through which an agent can act.

Positioning

Developed through a design-agent framework and tested in the DigitalFUTURES 2025 Landscape Agents workshop, the work positions architects and landscape architects not only as users of intelligent tools, but also as authors of the knowledge systems through which those tools operate.
