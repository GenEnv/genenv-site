AI + Landscapes

Rethinking landscape form through machine intelligence.

AI + Landscapes examines how deep learning changes the ways landscape architecture reads, models, and generates form. Building on the field's long engagement with systems, data, representation, and computation, the dissertation asks what happens when design tools learn from examples rather than operate only through predefined rules.

A new design lexicon

The research develops a new design lexicon organized around five relationships: landscape form follows data, image, process, simulation, and meaning. Together, these perspectives connect different forms of machine intelligence with long-standing landscape concerns, including environmental complexity, temporal change, perception, ecological processes, and interpretation.

Applied experiments

The dissertation brings this theoretical framework into applied experiments in streetscape analysis, generative planting design, and landscape ideation. Rather than positioning artificial intelligence as a replacement for design judgment, it presents machine learning as a means of working across objective and subjective knowledge while keeping ecological integrity, interpretation, and human responsibility central to the design process.
