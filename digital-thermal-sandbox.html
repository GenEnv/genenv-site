Digital Thermal Sandbox

Testing landscape strategies against urban heat.

Digital Thermal Sandbox investigates how planting and material decisions influence outdoor thermal conditions. As Canadian cities experience hotter summers, landscape architects need more accessible ways to understand how tree canopy, vegetation, pavement, and building materials interact to shape urban microclimates. The project combines environmental sensing, three-dimensional landscape modeling, and simulation to create an interactive environment for testing design-based “what-if” scenarios.

Living laboratories — Winnipeg & Vancouver

University campuses in Winnipeg and Vancouver serve as living laboratories for comparing different canopy forms, planting configurations, and surface materials. The research aims to connect measured environmental conditions with design exploration, allowing landscape architects to evaluate heat-mitigation strategies earlier in the design process. Its findings will be translated into practical guidance for climate-responsive landscape design.

Status & support

This work-in-progress project is supported by the Landscape Architecture Canada Foundation (LACF). The current stage focuses on developing and calibrating the digital workflow, building the interactive sandbox, and testing initial landscape scenarios. More details are coming soon.
